What is an anomaly?

A data point that is unusual relative to the right comparison group. A ₹25L Opportunity is unusual among SMB pilots; it may be ordinary among enterprise renewals.

What is an error?

An anomaly might be a perfectly valid exception. An error is a human/business judgment: incorrect, incomplete, or needing action.

What does ML add?

It finds patterns you did not write as rules. It does not turn missing access, bad extraction, or unclear business policy into truth.

North star: metadata tells us what must be true; history tells us what is usual; ML tells us what deserves attention; humans decide what is actually wrong.

Data, populations, and patterns

Before math, get the vocabulary right. These ideas drive every later choice.

Record, feature, target

Record: one Opportunity. Feature: a measurable input, e.g. Amount, StageName, Contract present. Target/label: the answer we want to predict, e.g. “real data issue.”

Amount = 120000Stage = Closed WonLabel = issue?

Population vs cohort

A population is all scanned records. A cohort is a comparable subgroup: Enterprise + Closed Won + FY26. Cohorts prevent apples-versus-rockets comparisons.

Always ask: “unusual compared with whom, during which time window?”

Schema vs observed data

Schema says a field exists and is a picklist. A profile says 42% of current records have it empty. Neither proves the business intended it to be complete for every cohort.

A field you cannot read is not blank. It is unknown. Suppress rules that depend on it.

Signal, noise, and base rate

If only 1 in 1,000 Opportunities is actually bad, a model that flags 100 records may look busy but can waste reviewers’ days. This is a rare-event problem.

precision = confirmed issues / findings shown
recall = confirmed issues / all real issues

In quality work, decide which failure hurts more: missing a serious issue (low recall), or overwhelming people with false positives (low precision).

Statistics: quantify “unusual” without pretending it is “wrong”

Use robust summaries first. They are simpler to debug and often beat fancy models on small, messy datasets.

Center and spread

Mean is the arithmetic average. Median is the middle value. A single ₹25L deal drags the mean; the median barely moves. For skewed business data, median and percentiles are usually more honest.

z-score = (value − mean) / standard deviation
IQR = Q3 − Q1
outlier if value < Q1 − 1.5×IQR or value > Q3 + 1.5×IQR

Z-score expects a roughly bell-shaped distribution. IQR is robust to extremes and is a better default for Amount.

Feature engineering: turn a record into useful evidence

A model only sees features you give it. This is often the most valuable and riskiest ML work.

Numeric features

Amount, days since CloseDate, child-record count. Scale numeric values when distance matters: one-hot StageName should not be dominated by Amount measured in rupees.

standardize: (x − mean) / std
robust scale: (x − median) / IQR

Categorical features

StageName and Segment are categories, not numbers. One-hot encoding turns “Enterprise” into a 0/1 column. Never encode categories as 1, 2, 3 unless order is genuinely meaningful.

Missingness is a feature

“Contract is blank” may itself be information. But distinguish known blank, not extracted, not readable, and not applicable. Collapsing them creates false findings.

Feature leakage: the silent cheater

Leakage happens when a training feature contains information unavailable at prediction time or effectively reveals the label. A model scores beautifully in testing and fails in reality.

Feature checklist

  • Is it available at the time of scanning?
  • Is it permitted by the field policy?
  • Does it mean the same thing across record types?
  • Could a user explain why it influenced a finding?
  • Can a null mean access failure rather than business data?

Unsupervised anomaly detection: Isolation Forest

Use this when you have few or no trusted labels, and want a ranked list of strange combinations—not a declaration of defect.

The intuition

Imagine repeatedly choosing a feature and a random split. An isolated point separates from its neighbours in fewer cuts than a dense-cluster point. An Isolation Forest grows many random trees and asks: “how quickly does this record become alone?”

It works well on mixed, high-dimensional feature sets after careful encoding, but it does not know business semantics or causal reason.

Output: an anomaly score. Not a probability that a record is bad, unless calibrated against human labels.

When not to use an anomaly model

Tiny cohort

With 12 records, the model mostly learns noise. Use rules and robust statistics.

Contaminated history

If historical bad data is common, “normal” may be broken. Review baseline records and monitor drift.

Unclear coverage

Models cannot repair missing objects, unreadable fields, or a mixed-time snapshot.

Association rules: learn “usually together” patterns

This is ideal for explainable conditional expectations like “Closed Won Enterprise opportunities usually have a contract.”

Three numbers, one rule

A → B
support = P(A and B)
confidence = P(B | A)
lift = P(B | A) / P(B)
  • Support: enough examples exist?
  • Confidence: among A, how often is B present?
  • Lift: is B more common with A than generally? Lift > 1 means positive association.
A high-confidence rule can be trivial if B is almost always true. Lift protects against that.

Metrics that matter

Precision asks, “when I interrupt a reviewer, how often am I right?” Recall asks, “of all real issues, how many did I surface?” F1 balances both, but business cost may demand a different balance.

precision = TP / (TP + FP)
recall = TP / (TP + FN)
F1 = 2 × precision × recall / (precision + recall)

Use a temporal holdout: train on older scans; evaluate on later scans. Never randomly shuffle time-dependent feedback.

Thresholds are product decisions

A “high severity” finding might require deterministic evidence plus high confidence. A low-confidence anomaly becomes a low-priority review candidate. Keep severity (business impact), confidence (evidence strength), and priority (what reviewers see first) separate.

Production ML: trust is a feature

These concepts prevent a beautiful dashboard from telling accidental lies.

Coverage contract

Every scan says which objects and fields were included, skipped, inaccessible, or unsupported. The UI must never imply “org-wide” when it saw one object.

Reproducibility & provenance

A finding stores data snapshot, metadata version, policy version, rule/model version, feature version, and scan ID. Ask: “Could we recreate this exact claim?”

Drift

Patterns change: new sales process, fiscal quarter, new picklist, acquisition. Monitor feature distributions and acceptance rates. Retrain deliberately, not automatically on every label.

Feedback loop

Reviewer labels are valuable but biased: people only label what the system showed them. Record sampling and assignment decisions, suppress repeated false positives, and periodically audit what the system never surfaced.

Model lifecycle

  1. Define policy and coverage.
  2. Collect baseline snapshots.
  3. Build rules and profiles.
  4. Evaluate candidate models on later data.
  5. Review and approve thresholds.
  6. Deploy with monitoring and rollback.
